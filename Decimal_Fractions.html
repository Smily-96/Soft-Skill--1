What Are Decimal Fractions?

▼
Decimal Definition

A way to represent parts of a whole using the base-ten system — a whole number part followed by a decimal point and digits representing a fraction of one.

0.5 = half  |  0.75 = three-fourths
Fraction Definition

A way to represent a part of a whole using a numerator (top) and denominator (bottom).

1/2 = one out of two equal parts
Decimal Fraction Definition

A fraction where the denominator is 10 or a power of 10 (10, 100, 1000, …). Because the denominator is always a power of 10, it can be written directly as a decimal.

0.a = a/10  |  0.ab = ab/100  |  0.abc = abc/1000
📝 Decimal Fraction vs. Non-Decimal Fraction:
1/10, 45/100, 34/1000 → decimal fractions (denominator is a power of 10)
2/5, 4/9, 21/489 → non-decimal fractions (denominator is not a power of 10)

Key Differences from Other Fractions

  • Simplicity: a base-10 denominator makes them straightforward to read and write.
  • Ease of comparison: lining up decimal points makes comparing values simple.
  • Convenience: ideal for money, measurements, and percentages.
Ex: Is 63/1000 a decimal fraction? Is 3/7?
  1. A fraction only counts as a decimal fraction if its denominator is exactly a power of 10 — 10, 100, 1000, and so on.
  2. Check 63/1000: the denominator 1000 = 10³, which is a power of 10.
  3. So 63/1000 is a decimal fraction, and it can be written directly as 0.063.
  4. Check 3/7: the denominator 7 is not a power of 10, and cannot be turned into one by any simple scaling.
  5. So 63/1000 is a decimal fraction, 3/7 is not
🔀

Types of Decimal Fractions

▼
Terminating Decimal Definition

Ends after a finite number of digits. Can always be written as an exact fraction.

0.5, 0.75, 0.25 — all stop after a few digits
Non-Terminating Decimal Definition

Continues indefinitely without ever stopping.

π ≈ 3.14159265… (goes on forever)
Recurring Decimal Definition

A non-terminating decimal with a digit or block of digits that repeats forever. Can still be written as an exact fraction.

0.3333… = 1/3  |  0.285714285714… = 2/7
Non-Recurring Decimal Definition

Non-terminating and the digits never settle into a repeating pattern. These are irrational — they cannot be written as an exact fraction.

√2 ≈ 1.41421356…  |  π ≈ 3.14159…
📝 Key Fact: Only terminating and recurring decimals can be converted to an exact fraction p/q. Non-terminating, non-recurring decimals (irrational numbers) cannot.
Ex: Classify 0.6, 0.3333…, and 1.41421356… (=√2)
  1. 0.6 has exactly one digit after the decimal point and then stops — this is a terminating decimal.
  2. 0.3333… has the digit 3 repeating forever in a fixed pattern — this is a recurring decimal.
  3. 1.41421356… (√2) never stops and never falls into a repeating block — this is a non-terminating, non-recurring (irrational) decimal.
  4. So: 0.6 → terminating, 0.3333… → recurring, √2 → non-terminating & non-recurring
📍

Place Value in Decimal Fractions

▼

Every digit's position relative to the decimal point gives it a specific value. Each place to the right of the decimal point is worth one-tenth of the place to its immediate left.

Position (right of decimal point)NameValue
1st digitTenths1/10
2nd digitHundredths1/100
3rd digitThousandths1/1000
4th digitTen-thousandths1/10000
Note: In 127.45, the "127" is the whole number part and ".45" is the fractional part — the digit 4 is in the tenths place (worth 4/10) and 5 is in the hundredths place (worth 5/100).
Ex: What is the place value of 7 in 12.674?
  1. Write out the number and look at the digits after the decimal point: 6, 7, 4.
  2. The first digit after the decimal (6) is in the tenths place, the second digit (7) is in the hundredths place.
  3. So the place value of 7 = 7 × (1/100) = 7/100.
  4. Answer: 7/100 = 0.07

Addition & Subtraction of Decimal Fractions

▼
  • Step 1: Line up the decimal points of the numbers being added or subtracted.
  • Step 2: Add trailing zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places.
  • Step 3: Add or subtract as you would whole numbers, keeping the decimal point in the same column.
💡 Tip: Always double-check the decimal points are aligned before adding or subtracting — this is the single most common source of error.
Ex: Add 12.4 + 0.75
  1. 12.4 has only one digit after the decimal, so add a trailing zero: 12.40.
  2. Line up the decimals: 12.40 and 0.75, decimal points in the same column.
  3. Add each column like whole numbers: 40+75=115, carry the 1: hundredths=5, tenths=1(carry)+4+7=... working it through gives 13.15.
  4. Answer: 12.40 + 0.75 = 13.15
Ex: Subtract 8.1 − 2.34
  1. 8.1 has only one digit after the decimal, so add a trailing zero: 8.10.
  2. Line up the decimals: 8.10 and 2.34, decimal points in the same column.
  3. Subtract each column, borrowing where necessary: 8.10 − 2.34.
  4. Answer: 8.10 − 2.34 = 5.76
✖️

Multiplication of Decimal Fractions

▼
  • Step 1: Ignore the decimal points and multiply the digits as whole numbers.
  • Step 2: Count the total number of decimal digits in BOTH original numbers.
  • Step 3: Place the decimal point in the product by counting that many digits from the right.
💡 Quick tip: To multiply by 10, 100, or 1000, just shift the decimal point right by as many places as there are zeros — e.g. 3.25 × 100 = 325.
Ex: Multiply 2.54 × 1.3
  1. Ignore the decimals for now and multiply as whole numbers: 254 × 13 = 3302.
  2. Count the decimal digits: 2.54 has 2, and 1.3 has 1. Total = 2 + 1 = 3.
  3. Place the decimal point 3 digits from the right of 3302: 3.302.
  4. Answer: 2.54 × 1.3 = 3.302
Ex: Multiply 0.125 × 0.16
  1. Ignore the decimals and multiply: 125 × 16 = 2000.
  2. Count the decimal digits: 0.125 has 3, and 0.16 has 2. Total = 3 + 2 = 5.
  3. Place the decimal point 5 digits from the right of 2000: 0.02000.
  4. Answer: 0.125 × 0.16 = 0.02 (trailing zeros dropped)
➗

Division of Decimal Fractions

▼
  • Step 1: Count the decimal digits in the divisor.
  • Step 2: Move the decimal point the same number of places to the right in BOTH the divisor and dividend, making the divisor a whole number.
  • Step 3: Divide as you would whole numbers, then place the decimal point in the quotient directly above its new position in the dividend.
Ex: Divide 3.24 by 1.2
  1. 1.2 has one digit after the decimal, so move the decimal one place right in both numbers: 3.24 → 32.4, and 1.2 → 12.
  2. Now divide as whole-ish numbers: 32.4 ÷ 12.
  3. 32.4 ÷ 12 = 2.7.
  4. Answer: 3.24 ÷ 1.2 = 2.7
Ex: A recipe calls for 3.2 cups of flour, but the only measuring cup holds 0.75 cups. How many full scoops are needed?
  1. 0.75 has two decimal digits, so move the decimal two places right in both numbers: 3.2 → 320, and 0.75 → 75.
  2. Now divide: 320 ÷ 75 = 4.2 (approximately).
  3. Since only whole scoops count, 4 full scoops give enough flour, with a little left over.
  4. Answer: 4 full scoops

Method 1: Multiply by a Power of 10

▼
  • Step 1: Count the number of digits after the decimal point.
  • Step 2: Multiply the decimal by 10 raised to that count, moving the decimal point all the way to the right.
  • Step 3: Write the resulting whole number over the matching power of 10, then simplify.
Ex: Convert 0.75 to a fraction
  1. There are two digits after the decimal point (7 and 5).
  2. Multiply by 10² = 100: 0.75 × 100 = 75.
  3. Write 75 over 100: 75/100.
  4. Simplify by dividing both by their greatest common divisor, 25: 75÷25=3, 100÷25=4.
  5. Answer: 0.75 = 3/4
Ex: Convert 0.625 to a fraction
  1. There are three digits after the decimal point (6, 2 and 5).
  2. Multiply by 10³ = 1000: 0.625 × 1000 = 625.
  3. Write 625 over 1000: 625/1000.
  4. The greatest common divisor of 625 and 1000 is 125: 625÷125=5, 1000÷125=8.
  5. Answer: 0.625 = 5/8
📐

Method 2: Using Place Value

▼
  • Step 1: Identify the place value of each digit after the decimal point (tenths, hundredths, thousandths, …).
  • Step 2: Write the whole run of digits after the decimal point as the numerator, over the matching place-value denominator.
  • Step 3: Simplify the resulting fraction.
Ex: Convert 0.75 to a fraction using place value
  1. 0.7 = seven tenths = 7/10, and 0.05 = five hundredths = 5/100.
  2. Combine using a common denominator of 100: (7×10+5)/100 = 75/100.
  3. Simplify by dividing both by 25: 75÷25=3, 100÷25=4.
  4. Answer: 0.75 = 3/4 — same result as Method 1
Remember: both methods always give the same equivalent fraction — pick whichever feels easier to you.
➖

Handling Negative Decimals

▼
  • Step 1: Drop the negative sign and treat the number as positive.
  • Step 2: Convert to a fraction using either method above.
  • Step 3: Reapply the negative sign to either the numerator or the denominator — never both.
⚠️ Misconception: Making both the numerator and denominator negative is wrong — the two negatives would cancel out and give a positive value again.
Ex: Convert −0.75 to a fraction
  1. Drop the negative sign: 0.75.
  2. Convert 0.75 to a fraction: 3/4.
  3. Reapply the negative sign to just one part.
  4. Answer: −0.75 = −3/4 (equivalently 3/−4)
🔁

Converting Recurring Decimals to Fractions

▼
Simple Decimal Definition

A decimal that terminates and never repeats — straightforward to convert with Method 1 or 2.

Recurring Decimal Definition

A decimal with a repeating digit or block. Needs a small bit of algebra to convert to a fraction.

  • Step 1: Let x equal the repeating decimal.
  • Step 2: Multiply x by 10ⁿ, where n is the number of digits in the repeating block, so the decimal point shifts past one full block.
  • Step 3: Subtract the original equation from the new one — this cancels the repeating part, leaving a whole number.
  • Step 4: Solve for x and simplify.
Ex: Convert 0.414141… to a fraction
  1. The repeating block "41" has 2 digits. Let x = 0.414141…
  2. Multiply both sides by 100 (since the block is 2 digits long): 100x = 41.4141…
  3. Subtract the original equation: 100x − x = 41.4141… − 0.4141… → 99x = 41.
  4. Divide both sides by 99: x = 41/99.
  5. Answer: 0.414141… = 41/99
Ex: Convert 0.142857142857… to a fraction
  1. The repeating block "142857" has 6 digits. Let x = 0.142857142857…
  2. Multiply both sides by 10⁶ = 1,000,000: 1000000x = 142857.142857…
  3. Subtract the original equation: 999999x = 142857.
  4. Divide both sides by 999999: x = 142857/999999, which simplifies to 1/7.
  5. Answer: 0.142857142857… = 1/7
